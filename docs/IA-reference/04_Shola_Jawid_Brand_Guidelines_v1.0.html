01 — BRAND FOUNDATION

A publication of record,
not a website.

The brand reads as a serious editorial institution: a journal with a point of view and a long archive. Three principles govern every visual decision below.

PRINCIPLE 01

Restraint

Three colours, two typefaces, generous whitespace. The brand earns its presence by leaving things out, not adding them.

PRINCIPLE 02

Weight

Heavy black mastheads, serif headlines at scale, strong typographic rules. Visual weight signals editorial seriousness.

PRINCIPLE 03

Meaning

Crimson is the eternal flame of the publication's name made visual. Every accent should mean something, never decorate.

05 — COLOUR HIERARCHY

The 60 / 30 / 10 rule.

Every screen — whether a homepage, an article, or a library shelf — should distribute the three primary colours in this ratio. It is the single most important rule in the system and the one that prevents the brand from drifting.

60% · PAPER WHITE
Dominant canvas. Reading surface. Never less than the majority of any view.
30% · INK BLACK
Typography, mastheads, footers, structural rules.
10% · CRIMSON
Accents, links, action.
60% — CANVAS

Paper White carries the brand. It is the reader's resting state and the surface against which black type and crimson accents earn their weight.

30% — STRUCTURE

Ink Black is the workhorse — body type, masthead, footer, decisive rules. It must dominate the typographic layer without overwhelming the canvas.

10% — ACCENT

Crimson is rationed. It signals action and identity in small, deliberate doses. The moment crimson begins to feel decorative, the system is broken.

07 — DO & DON'T

Rules with teeth.

The system breaks the moment Crimson is used decoratively, or whitespace gets crowded. These are the rules most likely to be violated under deadline pressure — protect them.

DO · Crimson as accent only
TOPICS · ECONOMY

Crimson appears only at small scale — labels, links, "current" markers. Black carries the headline.

DON'T · Crimson headlines
TOPICS · ECONOMY
A serious editorial headline reads like this

Never use Crimson for body text or headlines. It loses its signalling power and the page reads as decorative.

DO · Black masthead, crimson rule

Editorial content sits below.

The signature lock-up: black masthead anchored by a single crimson rule. Stable, weighty, recognisable.

DON'T · Crimson masthead
شعله جاویدAVOID
Loses gravitas; reads as agitational.

A red masthead breaks the heavyweight-press read. Crimson must always sit against black or white, never become the surface.

DO · Generous whitespace
Air around the type
does the heavy lifting.

Whitespace is structural, not empty. It carries the seriousness the brand promises and lets type lead.

DON'T · Crowd the page
AVOID PATTERNED COLOUR BLOCKS

Never tile or pattern the brand colours. This is editorial design, not corporate identity — patterns kill the read.

DO · Approved colour pairings only
Ink + White Crimson + White Tint + Deep White + Ink

Four pairings cover every UI need. Stick to them and the brand stays unmistakable.

DON'T · Introduce new colours
Blue? Teal? Gold?
NOT IN SYSTEM

Off-palette colour is the fastest way to weaken the brand. If a use case appears to require one, the use case is wrong.
